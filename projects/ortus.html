Summary

ORTUS was a twin-stick shooter developed by a team of 13 students over the course of 8 weeks. As the producer, I was responsible for guiding the team through the entire development process, from concept to completion.

  • Led the team in establishing a clear vision for the game during the concepting phase, ensuring alignment and focus from the start.
  • Structured the team into feature teams and created a team contract to define roles, responsibilities, and workflows.
  • Implemented and adapted Agile (Scrum) methodologies to fit the team's needs, managing the Product Backlog and overseeing sprint planning.
  • Ran daily stand-ups and bi-daily meetings to track progress, identify risks, and address challenges, keeping the team on schedule.
  • Acted as the primary communicator between the team and stakeholders, providing regular updates and maintaining transparency.
  • Proactively resolved conflicts and bottlenecks, including a major level design delay in week 5, by reorganising workflows and cutting scope to meet the deadline.
  • Improved team collaboration and internal testing by introducing stricter testing processes and better communication across feature teams.
  • Maintained team morale and engagement through regular one-on-one check-ins and open conversations, ensuring everyone felt supported and heard.
  • Delivered a polished game on time by balancing ambitious goals with realistic scope management and continuous risk assessment.
  • Managed a large Agile team, adapting workflows, and leading under tight deadlines.

On ORTUS

01Building the team

From the very beginning, I knew that a clear and unified vision would be critical to our success. With only 8 weeks to deliver a polished game, there was no room for misalignment or confusion. During the concepting phase, I led brainstorming sessions where we voted on the core idea for ORTUS. Once the direction was set, I worked with the team to establish a structure of feature teams, ensuring that each discipline — design, art, and programming — had clear ownership of their tasks.

To keep everyone on the same page, I drafted a team contract that outlined our goals, responsibilities, and workflows. This document became the foundation of our collaboration, helping us navigate the challenges of working in a large, multidisciplinary team.

02Using Agile

We adopted an Agile methodology, specifically Scrum, to manage our workflow. However, I quickly realised that Agile frameworks aren't one-size-fits-all. Without a designated Product Owner, the team collectively took on the responsibilities of prioritising tasks and managing the Sprint backlog. I stepped in to create and oversee the Product Backlog, ensuring that our goals were aligned with the timeline.

Our main tracking tool was a KanbanFlow board provided by the university, which became the heartbeat of our project. I tailored the Scrum process to fit our team's needs, holding daily stand-ups to review progress, identify blockers, and adjust priorities. This was my first time managing a sizable Agile team, and it taught me the importance of flexibility and adaptability in project management.

03Communication

As the producer, I prioritised clear and consistent communication. I led daily stand-ups and bi-daily meetings with discipline leads to review progress, assess risks, and tackle challenges. These meetings were essential for keeping the team aligned and ensuring everyone had a voice in the process.

I also made it a point to have informal one-on-one conversations with team members. These check-ins allowed me to gauge morale, address concerns, and provide support where needed. By fostering an open and collaborative environment, I was able to build trust and keep the team motivated throughout the project.

One of my key responsibilities was acting as the point of contact between the team and stakeholders. After each sprint, I shared updates on our progress, highlighting achievements and addressing any concerns through the use of data such as our burndown charts. This transparency helped maintain stakeholder confidence and ensured that everyone was working toward the same vision.

The most significant challenge was balancing the team's aspirations with the constraints of an 8-week timeline, and managing expectations while aiming for a polished final product, which required ongoing negotiation. However, we remained aligned with our vision, which stayed attainable throughout development.

04Challenges during development

Midway through development, we hit a significant roadblock. In the 5th week, it became clear that the level design was significantly behind. The issue stemmed from a cultural difference within the feature teams: while most teams had good quality-checking processes, the level design team had a more relaxed approach, which I had failed to notice earlier.

This was a pivotal moment for me as a producer. I immediately called a meeting with all the designers to assess the situation and determine a path forward. We cut nearly 80% of the planned levels to ensure the remaining content could be polished to a high standard. While this was a tough call, we decided to put quality over quantity, meeting our deadline and delivering a quality product.

To prevent similar issues in the future, I worked with the level design team to implement stricter quality checks and improve their workflow and communication. These changes paid off, and we didn't encounter any further setbacks.

05Music acquisition

One of my responsibilities during the development of ORTUS was to find music to complement our twin-stick shooter. After researching various tracks, I found a song that matched the game's tone. However, using copyrighted music in a student project presented its own set of challenges.

I contacted the song's producer directly, introducing our project and explaining our vision for ORTUS. Through clear and professional communication, I negotiated a deal that allowed us to use the track at no cost as long as we provided proper credits in the game. This agreement saved the project budget and ensured we adhered to legal standards.

Takeaways

As a producer, I set realistic goals by evaluating team capabilities and keeping the scope manageable to ensure a polished final product. While Scrum and Agile frameworks provide a strong foundation, I believe workflows should be tailored to fit each team's needs for better collaboration and efficiency.

Proactive risk assessment and a structured internal testing process help prevent roadblocks and keep development on track. Maintaining strong communication is just as important. Regular check-ins and open conversations create a positive work environment, resolve conflicts, and ensure everyone feels heard and supported.

Working with new tools and workflows strengthened my adaptability, problem-solving, and leadership skills. Every project presents unique challenges, but overcoming them has prepared me for future game productions with confidence.
